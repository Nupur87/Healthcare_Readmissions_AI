## 1. Key observed findings

- **Overall condition:** HIP-KNEE had the highest average ERR among the conditions reported, at **1.003985**, or approximately **0.4% above expected**.
- **State level:** Massachusetts had the highest average ERR, at **1.034428**, approximately **3.4% above expected**. Its average predicted rate was **14.908686**, compared with an average expected rate of **14.383138**, a difference of **0.525548 rate units**. This summary included **52 reporting hospitals**.
- **Massachusetts AMI:** The average ERR was **1.051164**, approximately **5.1% above expected**; the median ERR was **1.0285**, approximately **2.9% above expected**. Of **33 hospitals** with reported AMI ERR, **23 hospitals**, or **69.7%**, were above expected. The maximum ERR was **1.2639**, approximately **26.4% above expected**.
- **Ownership:** Proprietary hospitals had an average ERR of **1.012651**, approximately **1.3% above expected**, with a median ERR of **1.011725**, approximately **1.2% above expected**. The number of proprietary hospitals varied by condition, from **136 for CABG** to **502 for PN**. Average ERR was above 1 for proprietary hospitals across all six HRRP conditions.
- **Hospital rating:** Average ERR decreased as the rating increased:
  - 1-star: **1.045348**, approximately **4.5% above expected**
  - 2-star: **1.023037**, approximately **2.3% above expected**
  - 3-star: **1.005613**, approximately **0.6% above expected**
  - 4-star: **0.985902**, approximately **1.4% below expected**
  - 5-star: **0.964201**, approximately **3.6% below expected**
- The rating groups included **169 1-star, 567 2-star, 840 3-star, 758 4-star, and 287 5-star hospitals**.
- Across all conditions, **1-star hospitals had average ERR above expected**, while **5-star hospitals had average ERR below expected**. The largest rating-group gap was for HIP-KNEE: **1.108379** for 1-star hospitals, approximately **10.8% above expected**, versus **0.944577** for 5-star hospitals, approximately **5.5% below expected**.

## 2. Analytical interpretation

- The overall HIP-KNEE average was very close to the expected benchmark, despite being the highest among the conditions reported.
- The Massachusetts results indicate that the aggregate predicted readmission rate was higher than the aggregate expected rate among the **52 reporting hospitals**.
- The Massachusetts AMI distribution suggests that above-expected ERR was common among hospitals with reported AMI data, although the average and median indicate different aspects of that distribution.
- The ownership results show an association between proprietary ownership and average ERR above 1 in the reported data. This is an association, not evidence that ownership caused the observed ERR pattern.
- The rating results show a consistent descriptive gradient: higher rating groups had lower average ERR, with the 4-star and 5-star groups averaging below expected. This pattern may indicate an association between rating group and ERR, but it does not establish causality.
- The HIP-KNEE results show that the rating-group difference was particularly pronounced for that condition.

## 3. What cannot be concluded

- The metrics do not establish that any hospital, ownership type, state, or rating group caused higher or lower readmissions.
- They do not support conclusions about hospital quality or whether any hospital or group is “poor performing.”
- ERR describes performance relative to an expected benchmark; it does not by itself provide the absolute readmission rate. Absolute rates were supplied only for the Massachusetts state-level comparison, not for the other reported group comparisons.
- The data do not provide the number of hospitals contributing to every rating-by-condition or ownership-by-condition average. The available hospital counts also vary across conditions, so group averages may not be directly comparable without confirming denominators and aggregation methods.
- No uncertainty measures, confidence intervals, or inferential results were provided. The reported percentages and averages should therefore be treated as descriptive.
- The Massachusetts AMI findings are based on **33 hospitals with reported ERR**, including **23 above expected**, and may not represent hospitals without reported data.
- All conclusions apply only to hospitals with reported data and cannot necessarily be generalized to hospitals without reported metrics.

## 4. Recommended follow-up analyses

- Report the number of hospitals contributing to every ownership-by-condition and rating-by-condition average.
- Provide distributions by group and condition, including medians, ranges, and counts above and below expected, rather than relying only on averages.
- Compare absolute predicted and expected readmission rates alongside ERR for each condition, ownership group, and rating group where available.
- Recalculate group summaries using clearly documented aggregation rules and assess whether results change when each hospital is weighted equally versus when observations are weighted by the relevant reporting volume, if such denominators are available.
- Conduct descriptive sensitivity analyses for groups or conditions with relatively small numbers of reporting hospitals, particularly the Massachusetts AMI subset and the smaller ownership-by-condition groups.
- Examine whether the rating and ownership patterns remain similar when stratified by condition and state, while clearly labeling the results as descriptive associations.

## 5. Executive summary

Among hospitals with reported data, average ERR was highest for HIP-KNEE overall at approximately 0.4% above expected, while Massachusetts had the highest state average at approximately 3.4% above expected. Average ERR decreased consistently across hospital rating groups, from approximately 4.5% above expected for 1-star hospitals to 3.6% below expected for 5-star hospitals, with the strongest gap in HIP-KNEE. These are descriptive associations, and interpretation is limited by missing denominators, varying reporting counts, and the Massachusetts AMI sample of 33 hospitals.

1. Key observed findings
HIP-KNEE has the highest average ERR among the conditions provided, with an average ERR of 1.003985. This corresponds to predicted readmissions being approximately 0.4% above the expected benchmark on average for hospitals with reported data.
At the state level, Massachusetts (MA) has the highest average ERR, at 1.034428, across 52 reporting hospitals. This indicates predicted readmissions are approximately 3.4% above expected on average relative to the benchmark.
Massachusetts has an average predicted readmission rate of 14.908686 and an average expected rate of 14.383138. These are absolute readmission rates, whereas ERR describes performance relative to the expected benchmark.
For AMI in Massachusetts, the average ERR is 1.051164, corresponding to predicted readmissions approximately 5.1% above expected on average. The median ERR is 1.0285, or approximately 2.9% above expected.
Among the 33 Massachusetts hospitals with a reported AMI ERR, 23 hospitals (69.7%) have ERR > 1. Therefore, approximately seven in ten hospitals with reported AMI ERRs have predicted readmissions above their expected benchmark.
The maximum Massachusetts AMI ERR is 1.2639, corresponding to predicted readmissions approximately 26.4% above expected for the hospital represented by that maximum value.
2. Analytical interpretation

The results indicate that the average ERR for HIP-KNEE is only slightly above the expected benchmark, since its average ERR is very close to 1.

At the state level, Massachusetts shows a larger relative difference from expected, with an average ERR approximately 3.4% above the benchmark. The Massachusetts predicted and expected readmission rates also show that its average predicted rate is higher than its average expected rate; however, these absolute rates should not be interpreted interchangeably with ERR.

Within Massachusetts AMI, the average ERR of 1.051164 and the fact that 69.7% of hospitals with reported ERRs are above 1 suggest that above-expected predicted readmissions are present across a substantial proportion of the reporting hospitals rather than being represented solely by the maximum ERR.

The difference between the average AMI ERR of 1.051164 and the median of 1.0285 may indicate that some higher ERR values are influencing the average upward. This is an analytical interpretation and would require examination of the full hospital-level ERR distribution to confirm.

3. What cannot be concluded

These metrics do not establish why predicted readmissions are above expected. No conclusions can be made about clinical practices, patient characteristics, operational processes, or other potential drivers from the information provided.

The results also cannot be used by themselves to conclude that Massachusetts, HIP-KNEE cases, or individual Massachusetts hospitals have lower hospital quality. ERR represents readmissions relative to an expected benchmark and does not, on its own, establish overall hospital performance or causality.

The Massachusetts AMI analysis applies only to the 33 hospitals with reported AMI ERRs. Conclusions therefore should not be generalized to Massachusetts hospitals without reported AMI ERR data.

Similarly, the state-level Massachusetts result is based on 52 reporting hospitals. Without information about hospitals not represented in the data, the findings should remain limited to the reporting population.

The maximum AMI ERR of 1.2639 identifies an extreme reported value, but the supplied metrics do not show how many hospitals have similarly elevated values or the overall shape of the ERR distribution.

4. Recommended follow-up analyses
Examine the full distribution of Massachusetts AMI ERR values, including the concentration of hospitals at different ERR ranges. This would help determine whether the average is being influenced by a small number of relatively high observations.
Compare hospital-level predicted and expected readmission rates alongside ERR within Massachusetts AMI. This would preserve the distinction between absolute readmission levels and performance relative to expected.
Examine the 33 Massachusetts AMI hospitals over multiple reporting periods, if longitudinal data are available, to determine whether ERR > 1 is persistent or varies over time.
Compare Massachusetts AMI ERR patterns with those for other reported conditions within Massachusetts to determine whether the observed pattern is concentrated in AMI or appears more broadly.
Compare the Massachusetts AMI hospital-level distribution with the corresponding distributions in other states with sufficient reported data, while explicitly accounting for differences in the number of reporting hospitals.
5. Executive summary

Massachusetts has the highest state-level average ERR in the provided metrics at 1.034428, indicating predicted readmissions approximately 3.4% above expected across 52 reporting hospitals. Within Massachusetts AMI, the average ERR is 1.051164, and 23 of 33 hospitals with reported ERRs (69.7%) are above the expected benchmark. These findings identify areas for deeper hospital-level and longitudinal analysis but do not establish causality or hospital quality.

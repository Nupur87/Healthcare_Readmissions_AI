## 1. Key observed findings

- Among the reported conditions, **HIP-KNEE had the highest average ERR**, at **1.003985**, approximately **0.4% above expected**.
- **Massachusetts had the highest average state-level ERR**, at **1.034428**, approximately **3.4% above expected** among hospitals with reported data.
- In Massachusetts, the average predicted rate was **14.908686**, compared with an average expected rate of **14.383138**—a difference of **0.525548 rate points**. These are absolute rate measures and should not be conflated with ERR.
- For **Massachusetts AMI**, the average ERR was **1.051164**, approximately **5.1% above expected**.
- Among the **33 Massachusetts hospitals with reported AMI ERR**, **23 were above expected**, representing **69.7%**.
- The maximum reported Massachusetts AMI ERR was **1.2639**, approximately **26.4% above expected**.
- The Massachusetts state-level result includes **52 reporting hospitals**, while the Massachusetts AMI result includes **33 hospitals with reported ERR**.

## 2. Analytical interpretation

- The overall condition-level result suggests that HIP-KNEE was closest to expected among the reported conditions, while remaining slightly above the benchmark.
- The Massachusetts state-level ERR indicates that predicted readmissions were, on average, modestly above the expected benchmark for hospitals with reported data.
- The Massachusetts AMI results show a larger average relative difference from expected than the overall HIP-KNEE and state-level Massachusetts results.
- The fact that 23 of 33 reported Massachusetts AMI hospitals were above expected may indicate that above-expected ERR values were more common than below-expected values in this reported group.
- These patterns may warrant further examination of variation across hospitals and the relationship between absolute rates and relative-to-expected performance. These are analytical interpretations, not causal findings.

## 3. What cannot be concluded

- The metrics do not establish that any hospital caused higher or lower readmissions, or that any hospital is high- or low-quality.
- No causal explanation can be drawn from these results.
- The metrics do not provide patient characteristics, hospital attributes, clinical details, case volume, reporting completeness, or uncertainty estimates.
- The state-level and AMI results use different numbers of reporting hospitals—52 and 33, respectively—so they should not be treated as directly equivalent populations.
- The data do not show the distribution of ERR values across hospitals beyond the reported counts and maximum for Massachusetts AMI.
- The results may not generalize beyond hospitals with reported data.
- Sample-size limitations are relevant, particularly for the Massachusetts AMI analysis, which is based on 33 hospitals and may not represent all hospitals in the state.

## 4. Recommended follow-up analyses

- Quantify hospital-level variation in ERR for the reported Massachusetts AMI hospitals, including the median, quartiles, minimum, and distribution.
- Add uncertainty measures, such as confidence intervals or other reliability estimates, for hospital-level and aggregate ERR values.
- Examine hospital-level sample sizes or case volumes, if available, and assess whether aggregate results change when weighted by volume rather than by hospital.
- Compare absolute predicted and expected rates alongside ERR for each hospital or subgroup to distinguish differences in baseline rates from relative performance against expectation.
- Reconcile the differing reporting populations by documenting which hospitals contribute to the state-level analysis versus the Massachusetts AMI analysis.
- Repeat comparisons across conditions and states using consistent reporting criteria and sample-size thresholds, if those underlying data are available.

## 5. Executive summary

Among hospitals with reported data, Massachusetts had the highest average state-level ERR at approximately **3.4% above expected**, while Massachusetts AMI had an average ERR approximately **5.1% above expected**. For Massachusetts AMI, **23 of 33 reporting hospitals** were above expected, but the smaller reported sample and lack of hospital-level uncertainty measures limit interpretation. These findings describe relative-to-expected patterns only and do not establish hospital quality or causality.
